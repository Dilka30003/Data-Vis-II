THE SHIFT

From an option
to almost everyone.

In 2017, three in four adults used a phone to get online. By 2025, it was nearly universal. The biggest change is not among the youngest adults, but those who started further behind.

The mobile phone pulled ahead

Devices used to access the internet, Australian adults (%).

The shift is to mobile: phone internet use rose from about three in four adults in 2017 to almost everyone in 2025.

A shared doorway, different starting points. The age gap narrowed sharply, but access to a phone does not tell us what people do online.

Same connection.
Different digital lives.

Online habits are not uniform across generations. These views put phone use into context: websites, apps and activities may happen on a computer or tablet as well as a phone.

THE DIVIDE

A phone is a start.
It is not always enough.

The Australian Digital Inclusion Index (ADII) reports that 93.3% of Australians have a smartphone, yet 9.7% rely on a mobile-only connection. These are separate measures: ownership alone cannot tell us whether someone has the access, affordability and ability they need. ADII 2025 report ↗

Who relies on mobile alone?

Reliance is uneven: 18.8% of public-housing residents rely on mobile-only access, compared with 9.7% overall.

Nearly everyone can get online.
The quality of that connection still matters.

Mobile internet has become commonplace, but people's habits and circumstances still differ. The network supports participation; having a phone alone does not guarantee affordable access or the skills to use it.

Read the measures together, not as interchangeable statistics: widespread use is a starting point, not proof of equal digital inclusion.
